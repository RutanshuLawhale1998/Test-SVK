Solar geometry, scrolled

Sunset is
a geometry
problem.

Scroll becomes an hour angle. The sun's altitude follows from spherical trigonometry, and everything else — sky colour, cloud lighting, how black the trees go, even the tint of these panels — reads off that one angle.

Glass that knows the time

A frosted panel is unreadable against a bright noon sky and invisible against a night one, so the tint can't be a fixed value. These cards sample the live sky colour, push it dark, and scale their own opacity against sky luminance — denser at midday, nearly clear at midnight.

The border and the inset top sheen follow the same signal, which is why the edge catches warm light at sunset and cools to blue after.

Clouds are lit, not tinted

A cloud isn't a grey shape that turns orange at sunset. It's a volume lit from one side, and the direction of that lighting is the whole effect. The gradient across each deck points at the sun's actual screen position, so as the sun drops the illumination swings from overhead to raking side-light.

This is why a sunset cloud has a burning rim and a dark belly. Same cloud, two light sources: direct sun above, scattered skylight beneath.

Two decks

High cirrus catches the light first and holds it longest — thin ice at altitude stays in sunlight for minutes after the ground is in shadow, which is why the highest clouds are the last things still burning after sunset.

Lower cumulus goes into shadow early and stays dark. Both drift at their own rate, tiled seamlessly so there's no seam to find.

Trees on the ridge

The treelines are generated, not drawn. Each ridge is stored as a list of vertices, so tree bases sit at the interpolated height of the silhouette beneath them rather than floating near it.

Firs get stacked tiers with a slight lean; the nearest line mixes in broadleaf crowns and taller individuals for scale. Density and height fall off with distance, and each line is darker than the ridge behind it, because it's closer.

Air in between

Distant ridges are partly replaced by the atmosphere in front of them, so they shift toward the live horizon colour and lose contrast. As the sun sets that haze contribution drops away and the layers collapse toward silhouette — which is exactly when the trees stop being green and become shapes.

Night

Stars arrive in order of apparent magnitude, scintillating harder near the horizon where there's more air. The moon rose gibbous, with earthshine on its dark limb.
